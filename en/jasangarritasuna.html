Sustainability

Sustainable Development Goals (2030 Agenda): what we do, what we want to improve and how we will measure it.

Where we stand today

PCLUB is still in the project phase: the business is not open yet, so we have no real data on consumption, waste or emissions. That is why we treat this as a starting point: we have identified the sustainable features built into our business model and the areas that need improving, and we will measure baseline values once we start operating.

On the positive side, our business model helps by design: because many users share the same equipment, nobody has to buy a powerful computer and barely use it. Offering Ubuntu and free software reduces dependence on licences, and the website reduces the need to hand out information on paper. On the other hand, there are real risks: gaming computers use a lot of electricity, electronic equipment becomes special waste at the end of its useful life, and printing, 3D printing and the bar all generate waste.

Our 5 priority SDGs

Given what our business does, we have chosen five of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) as priorities. Select one to jump to its details.

  • 4 SDG Quality education
  • 7 SDG Affordable and clean energy
  • 8 SDG Decent work and economic growth
  • 12 SDG Responsible consumption and production
  • 13 SDG Climate action
4

SDG 4: Quality education

Goal: Give students and learners access to suitable computers and software, without having to buy the equipment.

Challenges

  • Powerful computers are expensive and many students do not have suitable equipment at home.
  • Students’ incomes are limited, so prices must be competitive.
  • Opening hours need to be flexible for exam and deadline periods.

Practical actions

  • Offer a reduced rate for students.
  • Have Ubuntu and free software pre-installed so people can learn with no licence costs.
  • Offer private rooms for study groups.
  • Run small workshops on Linux and office software.

Measuring results

  • Number of student users and hours used at the student rate, each month.
  • Number of workshops held and participants.
  • User satisfaction survey (from 1 to 5).
7

SDG 7: Affordable and clean energy

Goal: Reduce energy consumption and use renewable energy.

Challenges

  • Gaming computers use a lot of electricity, mainly because of their graphics cards.
  • The premises will be open for many hours and the machines will often be switched on.
  • Lighting and heating or cooling are also significant energy uses.

Practical actions

  • Choose an electricity supplier that guarantees renewable energy.
  • Set up automatic inactivity profiles (sleep and screen off) on the computers, on both Windows and Ubuntu.
  • Install LED lighting and motion sensors in the private rooms.
  • Give priority to highly energy-efficient equipment when buying.
  • Install consumption meters in the gaming area.

Measuring results

  • Electricity use (kWh) per month and per hour of use.
  • Share of renewable energy, each year.
  • Trend in the electricity bill.
8

SDG 8: Decent work and economic growth

Goal: Offer stable working conditions and support the local economy.

Challenges

  • Long hours and temporary contracts are common in hospitality and services.
  • A new business has small margins at the start.
  • Workload varies with how busy the club is.

Practical actions

  • Offer permanent contracts wherever possible.
  • Plan shifts in advance and respect rest breaks.
  • Train staff, both technically and in customer service.
  • Buy the bar’s products from local suppliers.
  • Ensure gender equality and equal opportunities when hiring.

Measuring results

  • Share of permanent contracts.
  • Training hours per employee each year.
  • Share of local suppliers.
  • Staff satisfaction survey and staff turnover.
12

SDG 12: Responsible consumption and production

Goal: Reduce waste and extend the useful life of our equipment.

Challenges

  • Gaming equipment becomes outdated quickly and there is strong pressure to replace it.
  • Electronic waste must be managed properly.
  • Printing can use a lot of paper, and 3D printing produces plastic waste.
  • Fast food generates a lot of packaging and food waste.

Practical actions

  • Carry out preventive maintenance and replace components (RAM, drives, graphics card) instead of changing the whole machine.
  • Hand over equipment at the end of its useful life to authorised electronic waste handlers, or donate it to schools and organisations.
  • Print double-sided by default, use recycled paper and preview before printing.
  • Use recyclable materials in 3D printing and test with small prints.
  • Use reusable cups in the bar, sort waste and forecast orders to cut food waste.

Measuring results

  • Average useful life of equipment (years) and number of machines repaired.
  • Electronic waste generated (kg) each year.
  • Sheets of paper printed and 3D printing waste, each month.
  • Waste sorting rate and food waste (kg).
13

SDG 13: Climate action

Goal: Measure and reduce our carbon footprint.

Challenges

  • Electricity will be the main source of our carbon footprint, and it is hard to reduce with gaming equipment.
  • Users’ journeys also produce emissions, and these are outside our control.
  • At the start we have no data to calculate emissions.

Practical actions

  • Calculate a baseline carbon footprint in the first year, using electricity and waste data.
  • Encourage sustainable transport: provide bicycle parking and publish public transport information on the website.
  • Optimise our IT infrastructure: use fewer, lighter servers and keep the website light (optimised images).
  • Raise users’ awareness, for example with posters and messages.

Measuring results

  • Carbon footprint (kg CO2e) each year.
  • Emissions per hour of use.
  • Share of users who travel sustainably, measured by survey.

Monitoring plan

To know whether our actions make a difference, we follow a monitoring cycle. One member of the team will be the sustainability officer, collecting the data and carrying out a review every year.

Until we open we have no baseline, so in the first year we will set starting values and compare results against them in the following years.

  1. Plan

    We set goals and actions for each SDG.

  2. Do

    We put the actions into practice.

  3. Measure

    We collect the data for each indicator.

  4. Improve

    We adjust our measures based on the results.

Conclusions

PCLUB’s business model, based on sharing computers and spaces, is a good starting point for sustainability. Even so, electricity use, electronic waste and the waste from the bar and from printing need special attention.

We have prioritised five SDGs (4, 7, 8, 12 and 13) and defined the challenges, practical actions and ways of measuring each one. Thanks to the monitoring plan, we will set baseline values in the first year and measure improvements in the following years.

Ultimately, PCLUB is a business that aims to put technology within reach of more people, and we want to do that while respecting the environment and society.
